About Kelak

Kelak is an Indonesian word meaning someday — a gentle, unhurried kind of later. Kelak is a place to leave something for that someday: a letter to a child not yet old enough to read it, a recording for an anniversary still years off, a note to your own future self.

We use a blockchain not because it is fashionable, but because a promise about time needs a keeper that does not forget, does not close, and cannot be persuaded to peek early. The contract holds the vault; the calendar holds the key.

How Kelak Works

I

Create

Write a letter, or paste a link to a photo, film or voice note meant for a particular someone.

II

Lock

Choose the day it may be opened. The vault is sealed on-chain and the key is time itself.

III

Open

When the date arrives, only the recipient's wallet can turn the lock and read what waited.

Why Blockchain?

Immutable

Once sealed, the contents cannot be altered or quietly deleted — not by us, not by anyone.

Time-Locked

The unlock date is enforced by the contract itself, not by a company's promise.

Ownership

The vault belongs to the wallets involved. No account, no subscription, no gatekeeper.
